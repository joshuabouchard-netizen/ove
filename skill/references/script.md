# Writing the script and shot list

The script is the spine of the video. Write it before touching any model and get the user's sign-off; every
later step (voice, visuals, captions, the critic's check) reads from it.

## Budget the words from the length

| Length | Calm explainer (about 145 wpm) | Energetic short (about 165 wpm) |
|---|---|---|
| 30 s | 65–75 words | 75–85 |
| 45 s | 100–110 | 110–120 |
| 60 s | 130–150 | 150–165 |
| 2 min | 270–300 | 300–330 |

Leave room: plan one or two beats of two to four seconds with music and picture alone, usually after the
opening and at the close. A cold open (an image and a sound, no voice) is a strong first two seconds.

## Two registers

**The lecturer** (the default for an explainer): a good teacher explaining something they understand to an
interested listener. Full sentences that connect; ideas introduced, developed and linked with "because",
"which means", "so"; one idea at a time; few numbers.

**The ride** (when the user wants energy, action, "not boring"): follow one thing on a journey (a drop of
water, a signal, a coin), first person where it helps; shorter sentences, verbs of motion, a reveal of scale,
a moment of tension and release, and a payoff that lands on the viewer ("you're breathing it right now").
Pair it with fast cuts (about 2.5 s a shot), first-person camera moves, slammed kinetic type on key words,
and sound design. Say plainly where drama bends the facts (a sap that "shoots up" really creeps).

Either way, avoid what models default to and listeners hear as machine-written: set-up/knock-down contrasts
("It's not X, it's Y"), triplets by reflex, rhetorical questions as transitions, number salads, hype words
(game-changing, unlock, seamless, journey), and summary sign-offs. Read the draft at speaking pace in your
head; rewrite anything you'd stumble over.

## Facts

An explainer that's wrong is worse than none. Check every claim against a source before the checkpoint and keep
a fact table under the shot list: claim, basis, and any careful wording (a figure that is "about half", a
"joins the air" rather than "is the air"). The user decides whether a dramatised line stays.

## Shot list

Split the narration into scenes of about 2–10 seconds, one idea each. For every scene decide:

- **Narration**: the exact words.
- **Visual source**: `gen` (AI clip), `still` (a keyframe with a slow push or pan), `motion` (Manim or HTML
  graphic: diagrams, charts, text, UI), `footage` (Creative Commons), or `reuse`.
- **Shot**: subject, action, camera move and speed, lens feel, light. One sentence a cinematographer would
  understand.
- **Characters and props** that must stay consistent, by reference-sheet name.
- **Duration**, from the narration, rounded to what the chosen model supports.
- **Sound**: the effect or music moment that lands on it, if any.

Write `script.md`: the shot table, the full narration in one block (the critic compares the final audio with
it), the fact table, and the cost estimate.

## Show the user before spending money

Send the script, the shot list and the estimate, and wait for a yes. Changing words now is free; changing them
after the voice and clips exist means redoing both.
