This drop of water is about to climb your plant, with no heart and no pump. It squeezes into a root, then shoots up a tube thinner than a hair. Up top, water escapes through tiny pores in the leaves, and because water molecules grip each other, every one that leaves yanks the whole chain up behind it. In the tallest redwoods, that chain is taller than a thirty-story building. Pull too hard, and it snaps, with a pop too high for you to hear. Most of the water blasts out into the sky. But sunlight rips a little of it apart, and the oxygen it frees? You're breathing it right now.
